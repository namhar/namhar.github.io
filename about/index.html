About

I’m Farrukh Rahman, an Applied Scientist at Microsoft and Head Instructional Associate for CS 7643 (Deep Learning) at Georgia Tech. I have over a decade of experience building and deploying machine learning systems across gaming, healthcare, and robotics.

At Microsoft, I work on imitation learning and computer vision for games, developing agents that act from pixel observations and deploying models to production scale environments. Previously, I worked on large scale anomaly detection at Aetna/CVS Health and on surgical robotics and electromechanical systems at Medtronic.

At Georgia Tech, I lead a team of 20+ teaching assistants for a course serving >400 students per term and develop curriculum spanning backpropagation/matrix calculus, vision transformers, diffusion models, and generative modeling.

Research

My research focuses on data efficient embodied AI systems that operate reliably under real world constraints. I’m interested in methods that reduce supervision, improve robustness, and translate effectively from research to deployment.

Data-Efficient Learning. Imitation learning and offline RL methods for improving sample efficiency and stability in high dimensional environments, including behavior cloning, dataset aggregation (DAgger), inverse/forward dynamics modeling, weak supervision, and self-supervised pretraining.

Embodied Perception & Decision-Making. Learning to act from raw sensory input in dynamic environments by integrating computer vision, temporal modeling, and goal-conditioned policies.

Production-Scale Learning Systems. Bridging research and deployment through scalable training pipelines, principled evaluation, and real time inference optimization, including hardware-aware tuning and model compression (e.g., pruning and quantization).
